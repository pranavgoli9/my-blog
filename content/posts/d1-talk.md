---

title: "Takeaways from Dan Sundheim's Masterclass"

date: "2026-10-23"

---

Yesterday, Dan Sundheim and several members of the D1 Capital team came to campus and hosted a masterclass for Wharton undergraduates. I was fortunate enough to be selected as a listener. Rather than summarize the ~2-hour presentation and Q&A, below are a few points I found particularly interesting:

1. **A repeated emphasis on management quality.** While this isn't unique to D1 (and is a very common investing criterion), I found the reasoning particularly insightful. On any given day, management makes several small, seemingly inconsequential decisions. Aggregated over time, however, those decisions create or destroy a lot of value, and a small edge in decision-making compounds. Roger Federer's 2024 Dartmouth commencement speech was referenced throughout the lecture as an example of this, as was Jeff Bezos' 1998 letter to shareholders.

2. **An explicit emphasis on the value of writing in prose.** This is a topic I've debated frequently with friends. The counterargument is that prose writing matters less when an LLM can do it for you. D1's view was that writing a pitch concisely forces you to boil it down to its key elements. If you can't sit down and write a brief summary of your pitch, it's likely too complicated.

3. **Dan himself said, "I don't care about catalysts,"** even contradicting the other D1 investors at the masterclass. He argued that focusing on a near-term catalyst often causes you to miss the forest for the trees, with the caveat that you must be willing to tolerate longer holding periods. This is consistent with his OCA short pitch on VIC, where he argues that although it may take time, the business will eventually go to zero.

4. **The other senior D1 investors listened intently the entire time, rarely checking their phones.** I found this notable. In my experience at other info sessions, people who aren't speaking are usually on their phones, particularly senior bankers and investors.
